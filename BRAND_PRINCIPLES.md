# Human Backstory — Brand Principles

## 1. Meaning before decoration

Every line, fracture, ring, fragment, shadow, and red accent should communicate the concept.

## 2. The threat comes from within

Human Backstory examines human mechanisms: brain, body, memory, attachment, evolution, social behavior. The visual tension should feel internal and revealing, not like an external monster or horror antagonist.

## 3. One red accent

`#C0413F` is not a generic brand highlight. It behaves like a wound, fracture, fault line, missing connection, or uncomfortable truth.

## 4. Darkness needs texture

The background is not a flat black rectangle. Grain, vignette, haze, or subtle atmospheric variation gives the surface depth.

## 5. Human tension, not theatrical fear

Faces may show tension, introspection, uncertainty, emptiness, vigilance, pain, or cognitive conflict. Avoid melodramatic screaming and horror tropes unless a specific concept genuinely requires them.

## 6. Feed-level consistency without template fatigue

Human Backstory should be recognizable as a collection. Recognition comes from typography, tonal treatment, red-accent logic, grain, contrast, and conceptual devices—not from placing every subject and headline in the same location.

## 7. Scientific restraint

Bold packaging may create curiosity, but explanatory graphics should be minimal, clear, and evidence-aware.

## 8. Canon is not guesswork

If the brand source does not define a value, do not present a guess as canon. Mark it provisional and keep it replaceable.
