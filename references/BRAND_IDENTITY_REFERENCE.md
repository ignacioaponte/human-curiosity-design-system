# Human Backstory — Brand Identity Reference

This file transcribes the operationally relevant content from the approved Human Backstory Brand Identity v1 so Claude Design can use it even when the binary PDF is not available through the GitHub connector.

## 01 Primary Wordmark

Human Backstory uses **Barlow Condensed 900**, uppercase, near-flush tracking.

The O in BACKSTORY is cut and offset four units, with a tapered red hairline in the gap.

The red accent is structural rather than decorative.

## 02 Channel Mark — The Fractured O

Avatar source: square 800×800, black ground.

At 24px the seam collapses to a single pixel of red and the ring must still read as broken.

The mark is the same construction as the O in the wordmark so the two reinforce each other.

## 03 Channel Banner

Export target: 2560×1440 at 2× source.

Wordmark and tagline live inside a 1546×423 safe area so they survive mobile and TV cropping.

Outside the safe area: constellation trace and vignette carry atmosphere only; no critical information.

Grain sits over everything as the final layer.

Tagline: **WHY ARE HUMANS LIKE THIS?**

## 04 Specification

### Ground
- `#0A0A0A`
- vignette: `#060606`

### Type & Accent
- primary light: `#F2F2F3`
- accent red: `#C0413F`
- one accent

### Typeface
- Barlow Condensed
- weight 900
- applications: wordmark, thumbnail text, evidence labels, on-screen AI text
- uppercase throughout these applications

### Clear space
- 1× cap height minimum margin on all four sides of the wordmark
- never place the mark on a flat field; ground always carries grain

## 05 Thumbnail Reference — BORN UNFINISHED

Title: Why are Human Babies Born So Useless Next to Chimpanzees.

Thumbnail text names the sensation rather than the mechanism.

One red accent is spent on the fracture rather than on a word.

Subject sits right and cropped; text holds negative space left.

Photography: hard single-side light, no saturation, B&W or cold duotone.

Copy: **BORN UNFINISHED**

## 06 Thumbnail Reference — STILL ON YOUR SKIN

Title: Why Your Body Secretly Kept the Fur You Thought You Lost.

Same family as 05: big word, qualifier beneath, fracture through frame, mark bottom-left.

Fracture mirrors 05 instead of repeating it.

Subject brief: forearm or shoulder skin, raking side light so fine vellus hair catches as a rim.

Copy: **STILL ON YOUR SKIN**

## 07 Thumbnail Reference — IT NEVER CLOSED

Title: Why You Are Wired to Never Let Go.

The subject is the open loop, so the frame device is a ring that almost closes instead of a fracture.

Composition is mirrored from previous references: subject left, type right.

Two concentric rings each contain a gap that never joins. They sit at low opacity so the red word remains the only loud element.

Subject brief: face half in shadow, eyes open and lit, looking past lens.

Copy: **IT NEVER CLOSED**

## 08 Thumbnail Reference — YOU WERE TOLD

Title: Why We Can't Remember the First Years of Our Lives.

Subject returns right, type left.

Device: a missing run in a timeline. The memory is not faint; it was never written.

A tick-marked timeline runs across frame, with the earliest stretch represented as a broken red line ending in a hard stop.

Copy: **YOU WERE TOLD**

Subject brief: hand holding an old child snapshot, or toddler face slightly out of focus.

## 09 Thumbnail Reference — BUILT WRONG

Title: Why Your Lower Back Never Forgives You for Standing Upright.

Subject left, type right.

Device: vertebral ticks follow the spine S-curve down the seam between photo and type. The lumbar three turn red and split.

Subject brief: bare back from behind, hard side light carving the spine, hand pressed to lower back.

Copy: **BUILT WRONG**

## 10 Thumbnail Reference — IT STILL HURTS

Title: Why Your Brain Makes Rejection Hurt So Much.

Text locked to left third; subject fills right two-thirds.

HURTS stays whole while its lower edge is chipped away and red glass-like fragments fall beneath it.

Subject brief: person alone, head lowered into one hand, hard side light.

Copy: **IT STILL HURTS**

## Interpretation rule

These examples define a family, not six rigid templates.

Claude Design must learn:
- alternating layouts
- one conceptual device
- one red accent
- psychologically meaningful negative space
- hard light
- near-monochrome treatment
- strong condensed type

It must not reduce Human Backstory to “black background + red crack.”
